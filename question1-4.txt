1. Explain the differences between var, let, and const in JavaScript 
regarding variable scope, hoisting, and reassignment.

var: Function-scoped, hoisted and initialized as `undefined`, and can be reassigned.
let: Block-scoped, hoisted but not initialized until its declaration, and can be reassigned.
const: Block-scoped, hoisted but not initialized until its declaration, and **cannot be reassigned**.


2. What are the primitive data types available in JavaScript, and how do they 
fundamentally differ from reference types like arrays and objects?


    Primitives store their actual value, while arrays and objects are reference types, meaning
     variables hold a reference to the data rather than the data itself.


3. Discuss the difference between loose equality (==) and strict equality (===). 
    Why is strict equality generally considered a best practice?

    == compares values after type conversion, while === compares both 
    value and type without conversion.

   === is generally preferred because it avoids unexpected results 
    caused by automatic type conversion
